# Phase 3 — Off-site citation building (the real lever)

This is the **dominant lever** — where the result is mostly won, and where most competitors won't do the work — your edge *(it moves AI recommendations far more than any on-site tweak; treat that as a directional rule of thumb, not a measured split — don't quote a number to clients)*. The mechanism: AI recommends entities that appear, with consistent framing, across **many trusted third-party places**. That feeds both the retrieval path (the engine searches and finds you cited) and the memory path (repeated mentions enter training data). It's the same underlying logic as backlinks — earned, not bought.

This is a **sustained program, not a one-off** — frame it to the client as the retainer. Run three fronts in parallel, ranked by ROI.

> The strongest off-site asset is **original data nobody else has** (Phase 2). Every front below works far better when you lead with a citable number instead of asking for a favor. Build the data asset first; it's the bait for all three fronts.

---

## Front 1 — Community (Reddit + niche forums) — highest ROI

Google and OpenAI have data deals with Reddit, and LLMs cite Reddit heavily. A genuinely helpful answer that happens to reference the brand's data is the single most cost-effective citation you can earn. But these communities are ruthless about self-promotion — break the rules and you lose the account and the brand's reputation.

### Hard rules (violate one and it backfires)
1. **Build credit first.** A new account answers only — no links — for the first 1–2 weeks. Strict subs (e.g. r/PersonalFinanceCanada) auto-remove new self-promoters.
2. **Answer-first, link-as-source.** The numbers/answer go in the comment body. The link is just "source/verify" — the answer must stand on its own without it.
3. **Frequency.** Link-bearing answers ≤ 1/day/subreddit; pure-text answers unlimited.
4. **Removed once → go text-only** in that sub (still brand-valuable), retry links in two weeks.
5. **Never start promo threads.** Only answer others' questions. A site link in your profile bio is fine.

### Find & vet the subreddit (before you ever post)
Don't assume which subs — find and qualify them first:
- Surface where the questions are actually asked: search `site:reddit.com <your target question>` on Google, and Reddit's own search sorted by new.
- **Read each sub's rules + wiki** for self-promotion limits — many enforce a 9:1 "mostly-not-your-own-stuff" ratio, an outright promo ban, or link restrictions; and check **account-age + karma gates** before you post (new/low-karma accounts get auto-removed).
- Picked the wrong sub once (removed) → go text-only there (still brand-valuable) and retry links in ~2 weeks.

### Daily 10-minute workflow
Keep a set of saved searches (sorted newest + relevant) for the subs where the target questions get asked. Pick 1–3 threads that have a real question and no good answer yet, and answer with the brand's real data.

### Answer template (generalize per niche)
> [Direct answer with the specific number first.] At $70,000 in Ontario you'd take home roughly **$53,600/yr (~$4,470/mo)** after federal + provincial tax, CPP and EI.
>
> [One line of useful context — the "why" or a comparison.]
>
> Source / check your exact figure: [link to the exact page that proves the number]

The pattern is archetype-independent: **lead with the number, add context, link as proof.** For a SaaS, the "number" might be a benchmark or a feature-comparison fact; for ecommerce, a real spec or price comparison. Use a **real human account with a real email** — the client's, not a sock puppet.

### Also worth it
- **Niche forums** with looser link rules than Reddit (e.g. RedFlagDeals for Canadian finance, indie-hacker forums for SaaS).
- **Quora** answers to questions in the niche.
- **Hacker News / Slack/Discord communities** for B2B/SaaS (same value-first rules).

## Front 2 — Original-data outreach (editorial citations) — highest quality

The goal is **real, editorial mentions that carry weight** — a journalist or respected blog quoting the brand's data and linking it. Ten genuinely relevant citations beat a hundred directory links. **Lead with data; never beg for a link.**

### ⛔ Never touch (these get domains penalized — worst during a new site's sandbox)
- Generic "write for us / submit guest post / paid inclusion" guest-post farms.
- Fiverr link sellers, PBNs, link-exchange groups, mass low-quality directory blasts.
These are link schemes Google explicitly targets.

### 2a. Journalists / press (free, top quality)
Register the founder on journalist-request platforms and respond — as the brand's expert — to relevant queries, attaching the brand's data article as the source. One mention in a real outlet beats 100 directory links.
- **Featured.com** (HARO's successor), **Qwoted**, **SourceBottle** (region-dependent).

**Response template (1–2 sentences they can quote verbatim):**
> [Direct, quotable answer leading with a concrete number.] *"In 2026 a full-time minimum-wage worker in Nunavut keeps $34,098 vs $26,478 in Alberta — a $7,620 gap."* Source: [link to your data page]. — [Name], [one-line credential].

**Rules:** hit the deadline (queries close in hours), send **one tight pitch** not a wall, be a **genuine expert** on that exact topic, and **never fabricate a figure or a credential**. Skip pay-to-pitch "get featured" upsells and AI-mass-pitching — both get you blacklisted by the journalists who actually matter.
- Respond fast, lead with a concrete data point, attach the citable URL.

### 2b. Resource-page & tool-list inclusion (scalable, mid-quality)
Ask relevant sites to add the brand to an existing "tools/resources" list — **not** a guest post, just a useful addition. Find their contact/about email, reference a specific article of theirs (proof you read it), and pitch the tool/data as useful to *their* readers.

### 2c. Listicle inclusion ("best [category]") — directly moves AI answers
"Best [category]" articles are exactly what LLMs read to answer "what's the best X". Find the ones ranking for the target queries, identify the author, and request inclusion — strongest when you can offer a reason they'd want you in (unique feature, original data, free tier). Getting into the listicles your competitors are in is often the fastest AI-visibility win.

### Pitch templates

**A — Data citation (to a blogger/journalist; strongest):**
> Subject: [Specific] data, free to cite
>
> Hi [Name], I run [Brand]. I just published original data on [topic] — e.g. [concrete, surprising stat with two numbers]. If it's useful for your readers it's free to cite or republish with a link: [URL]. Happy to pull a custom figure for [their angle] if helpful. — [Name]

**B — Resource/tool-list inclusion:**
> Subject: A free [tool] for your [readers/resource list]
>
> Hi [Name], loved your guide on [specific article]. For readers [doing the thing], I built [tool] — [one differentiator, e.g. free, no signup, covers X]. If you keep a tools/resources list it might be a useful add: [URL]. Either way, thanks for the great content. — [Name]

### Outreach rules
- **Personalize every email** — reference one specific piece of their work. Mass-merge templates are obvious and deleted.
- **Lead with value** (their readers benefit), not "please link me".
- **One at a time**, not blasts. **Follow up once** at day 7, polite close at day 21 — max two touches.
- **Real person, real email** (the client's). Log every send.

## Front 3 — Entity & authority signals

> ### 🧭 Two layers, and the mistake that comes from mixing them
>
> **Entity layer** — Wikidata, encyclopedias, map records, company/product databases. These
> change what the machines *believe the brand is*.
> **Content layer** — Reddit, 知乎, Quora, YouTube, Medium, 小红书. These add *citable
> third-party sources*.
>
> **Publishing a post is not correcting an entity.** An operator who posts ten answers and
> reports "we improved the knowledge graph" has done real work on the wrong axis — the
> entity is still whatever the graph said last week. Track them separately, report them
> separately, and when a client's problem is *Present & WRONG*, only the entity layer fixes it.
>
> ### And check ACCESS before promising anything
>
> "Add us to X" means a different thing on each platform, and the difference is what a
> retainer gets judged on:
>
> | Access | Means | Examples |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Yours** | just do it | site schema, YouTube |
> | **Open edit** | edit freely, community rules apply | Wikidata, OSM, Reddit, 知乎 |
> | **Claim + verify** | claim a listing, then you control it | GBP, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Crunchbase, G2 |
> | **Suggest only** | you propose, someone else decides | Wikipedia (with a declared COI), Google Knowledge Panel |
> | **Application** | gated, can be refused | 百度百科, 微信公众号 |
>
> Never promise a client an edit that is actually a suggestion. The full per-archetype list,
> filtered by market, is implemented in the console at `lib/entity-surfaces.ts` — keep the two in sync.


- **Wikidata item.** Easier than Wikipedia, and LLMs read Wikidata. Create an item with: a label + one-line description, **`P31` (instance of: human / business / website)**, **`P856` (official website)**, **`P159` (headquarters)**, **`P571` (inception)**, **`P2541` (operating area)**, **`P1454` (legal form)**, and **external-ID properties** (LinkedIn, Crunchbase, GitHub) as `sameAs` anchors — each statement **referenced** to a source. Use the canonical entity sentence verbatim.

  **🔴 Do not apply Wikipedia's notability bar here — they are different standards, and confusing them will make you refuse work you should be doing.**

  | | Wikipedia | **Wikidata (WD:N)** |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Needs | Independent, secondary, substantial coverage | **"Serious and publicly available" references — that is all** |
  | Company registry as a source | ✗ Not enough | **✓ Qualifies** |
  | Practical effect | Most SMB clients fail | **Any properly registered business passes** |

  **A government business registration is a qualifying source.** Pull the registration and
  reference the statements to its public record:

  | Where | Public lookup |
  | --- | --- |
  | 🇨🇦 BC | `orgbook.gov.bc.ca/entity/<REG#>` — open API, no login |
  | 🇨🇦 federal | Corporations Canada search |
  | 🇺🇸 | Secretary of State business search (per state) |
  | 🇬🇧 | Companies House (open API) |
  | 🇨🇳 | 国家企业信用信息公示系统 |
  | 🇦🇺 | ABN Lookup |

  ⚠️ The real deletion risk is a **bare promotional item with no sourceable statements at all** —
  not a small company. Reference the registration and it stands.

  **The item is only half the job: `sameAs` must point back.** Wikidata asserting who the client
  is, with nothing on the client's own site agreeing, is an unverified *claim*. Add the Wikidata
  URL to the site's `Organization.sameAs` (and the registration number as an `identifier`) so the
  assertion runs **both directions** — that is what turns it into a fact an engine will repeat.
  Worth the most on brands whose name collides with a bigger entity (see the name-collision check
  in Phase 0). *(Wikipedia only if genuinely notable with independent coverage — don't force it.)*
- **"Best of" lists & roundups** — see 2c; also a strong authority signal.
- **Quality directories & profiles** relevant to the niche (G2/Capterra for SaaS, Product Hunt for tools, industry associations for services) — selective, real ones only.
- **Google Business Profile** (local/service brands) — claim it; set category, service area, website, photos, services. Major for "[service] in [city]" and Maps. Seed early, **genuine** reviews. Keep NAP (name/address/phone) identical to the site and every directory. ⚠️ **Never fake/incentivize reviews or keyword-stuff the GBP name** — both get the profile suspended. Re-verify every entity property (name, role, URL, `sameAs`, Wikidata) is **identical** to the entity sentence in `onsite-sop.md` — any mismatch splits the entity.
- **Partner/client credit links** — designers, vendors, and clients you work with linking back to the same entity + URL.
- **Get listed where the engines already look.** Pull the *cited-domains* for your target prompts (the baseline / `measurement.md` shows which sites AI actually cites in your niche) — those listicles, directories and aggregators **are** the retrieval set. Claim / submit / correct your entry on each, keeping NAP + the entity sentence identical to your site so they reinforce (not split) the entity.
- **If the brand *itself* is a directory/aggregator** (see `archetypes.md`): the play inverts — you want to *be* the cited list. Get the directory into "best [category] directories" roundups, earn press for the breadth/freshness of your coverage, and offer your aggregated data (where you have the rights) as a citable resource.

### Platform traps that cost more than the listing is worth

Each of these has been the reason someone's work got reverted, flagged or banned. Read before touching the platform, not after.

- **Wikipedia — never edit a client's article yourself.** Undisclosed paid editing gets accounts blocked and the article tagged, which is *worse than no article*. Declare the conflict of interest and file an edit request on the Talk page. And if independent sources don't exist, don't create it: a deletion discussion is a permanent, public, indexable negative.
- **Wikimedia Commons — the logo is a licensing decision, not a task.** Uploading releases the file under a free licence anyone may reuse commercially, competitors included. Get the client's written sign-off. (A plain wordmark may fall below the threshold of originality and carry no copyright at all — that one is safe; a designed logo is not.)
- **OpenStreetMap — never edit it for SEO.** The community reverts promotional edits and blocks accounts. Add only what physically exists, tag it properly, stop.
- **Google Business Profile — the name field is not a keyword field.** Stuffing it, or incentivizing reviews, suspends the profile. Also: request access on call #1, verification runs weeks and blocks the whole entity loop.
- **Trustpilot — invitations must go to every customer**, not a filtered happy subset. Selective invitation breaks their rules and gets the profile publicly flagged, which is far worse than a mediocre score.
- **G2 / Capterra — reviews are identity-verified.** Never solicit only-satisfied customers or offer anything of value; detection lands the penalty on the client's listing.
- **Crunchbase — free editing is limited** and many fields sit behind a paid tier. Check what free still allows before promising a complete profile.
- **Google Knowledge Panel cannot be created**, only claimed once it appears. The work that makes it appear is upstream (schema, Wikidata, consistent profiles). Treat the panel as a **result you measure**, not a task you do.
- **百度百科 needs real published sources** and commercial entries usually go through the enterprise channel; it can be refused. Budget weeks.
- **360百科 / 搜狗百科 — verify before promising.** Whether open editing still exists, whether commercial entries are accepted, and whether pages are still indexed all change. Attemptable, never deliverable.
- **Hacker News punishes anything that reads as marketing**, and asking for votes is the fastest way to be flagged.
- **Reddit — check the sub's self-promo ratio and account-age/karma gate first.** A removed post is wasted work; a banned account loses the channel.
